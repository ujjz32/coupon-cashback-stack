## Why the code you found doesn't work

Coupon codes have a short shelf life. A merchant runs a promotion for a weekend, hands the code to its affiliate partners, and switches it off on Monday. Aggregator sites don't always switch it off with them. Some list a code for years after it died, because a dead code still gets traffic, and traffic still pays.

There's a second problem that costs more than a failed paste: **how referral credit gets assigned.** When you arrive at a store through one link and then apply a code from a different source, the store's affiliate system has to decide who gets credit for your order. Usually, the last tracked source wins. A code from a random coupon site can overwrite the tracking from wherever you started. If you clicked through a cashback platform first, that click-through was the thing that was going to pay you back — and the coupon site just took it.

So the two failures are connected. The codes most likely to be dead are also the ones most likely to break the mechanism that returns money to your account.

## Three discounts, three different wallets

Here's the part that makes the whole subject easier to think about. A single purchase can earn on three layers, and none of them come out of the same pocket:

- **The store's sale price** — funded by the merchant's pricing decision.

- **The promo code** — funded by the merchant's marketing budget.

- **Cashback** — funded by the commission the merchant pays an affiliate platform for sending you there, a share of which comes back to you.

- **Your card rewards** — funded by your card issuer.

Four, if you count credit card points. The important bit: they stack. A 20% promo code does not shrink your cashback, and your cashback does not cancel your card rewards. The reason people assume they can't be combined is that badly built coupon sites train you to treat them as competing offers.

## What actually breaks cashback

Most "cashback never paid me" stories trace back to one of these. None of them require support tickets if you avoid them upfront.

- **Navigating straight to the store.** No click-through, no attribution. Buying from a store you've used ShopBack for before doesn't help — each order needs its own click-through.

- **Applying a promo code from another coupon site mid-checkout.** This is the one that surprises people.

- **Ad blockers and privacy extensions.** They can strip the tracking tag. Whitelist the platform and the merchant's domain, or use the mobile app instead.

- **Switching browsers or tabs during checkout.** The tag lives in the session that created it.

- **Clicking through in a browser and then buying inside the merchant's app.** The session doesn't follow you across.

- **PayLater purchases** — these have been excluded from referral-bonus qualifying requirements since March 2024.

- **Buying excluded items.** Categories like gift cards or sale items are carved out at specific merchants. The terms sit on the merchant page; they're worth ten seconds.

## How long the money takes, and where it goes

Cashback moves through four states, and knowing them removes most of the anxiety:

| State | What it means | Typical timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tracked | The click and order were recorded | Within hours |
| Pending | The order is logged; the retailer hasn't confirmed it yet | Retail: a few weeks. Travel: until after the trip |
| Confirmed | The return window closed and the sale was verified | Set by the merchant's own returns policy |
| Withdrawable | Ready to move to your bank account or PayPal | Any time after confirmation |

Pending cashback can reverse to zero if the order is cancelled, returned, or turns out to contain excluded items. That isn't a tracking bug — a share of a sale that unwound stops existing. Confirmed cashback, once it's there, has no deadline to withdraw, and standard payouts carry no withdrawal fee.

If an order never tracks at all, or stays pending well past that merchant's normal window, file a missing cashback claim from inside your account with the order number, date and amount.
